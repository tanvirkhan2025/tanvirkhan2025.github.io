5 Timing Tricks for GRE Quant You're Probably Not Using

On paper, GRE Quant gives you about 1 minute 45 seconds per question. In practice, that number is useless — some questions take 30 seconds, others take three minutes, and the students who score well aren't the ones who solve faster. They're the ones who know when to stop trying.

1. Set a 60-second checkpoint, not a finish-line clock

Instead of watching the section timer, glance at your progress every 60 seconds on a question. If you're not clearly closer to an answer by then, that's your signal to guess strategically, flag it, and move on. Waiting until you "feel stuck" costs far more time than a fixed checkpoint.

2. Do the free ones first, on purpose

Quantitative Comparison questions are frequently faster than multi-step word problems, but students often solve in question order out of habit. On your first pass through a section, prioritize QC and questions you can visually estimate. Bank the easy points before a hard problem eats your buffer.

3. Estimate before you calculate

Before setting up an equation, ask what the answer should roughly look like. On multiple-choice and multi-select questions, this alone eliminates answer choices in seconds and often tells you whether a full calculation is even necessary.

4. Use the on-screen calculator only when arithmetic is the bottleneck

The calculator is for arithmetic, not for thinking. Students who reach for it before setting up the problem tend to lose the thread of what they're actually solving for. Set up the equation on paper first; calculate last.

5. Protect your last five minutes

With roughly five minutes left in a section, stop attempting new hard problems from scratch. Switch to reviewing flagged questions and picking off anything solvable with an estimate or elimination. A rushed guess on an unfamiliar problem is rarely worse than leaving it blank — but a rushed guess replacing a question you could have gotten right with more care is the real cost.
